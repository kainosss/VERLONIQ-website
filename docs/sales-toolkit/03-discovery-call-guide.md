# Business Clarity Call guide — free 20-minute call

**Purpose:** understand how the business handles new enquiries, decide together whether automation would genuinely help, and agree on a next step. Listen more than you talk. If it isn't a fit, say so.

## Agenda (20 minutes)

| Time | Section | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–2:00 | Welcome and purpose | "This is a free 20-minute call to understand how enquiries come in and whether automation would actually help. No obligation." |
| 2:00–10:00 | Their current process | Core questions plus the trade-specific set below |
| 10:00–15:00 | Optional 5-minute demo | Only if useful. Follow the [demonstration script](06-demo-script.md) and say clearly that it's a simulation |
| 15:00–18:00 | Fit and next step | Qualification check (below); suggest a package or say it's not a fit |
| 18:00–20:00 | Close | Confirm the next step and what you'll send; book scoping if qualified |

Take notes straight into the qualification sheet below. Send a recap within 24 hours.

---

## Core questions (all trades)

**How enquiries arrive**
1. Where do new enquiries come from? Website form, email, phone, text messages, Facebook or Instagram, Google Business Profile, Hipages-type lead sites, builders or real estate agents?
2. Roughly how many new enquiries do you get in a normal week? And in a busy week?
3. Who reads them first, and when? Office staff, the owner on the tools, or whoever gets to it?
4. What happens to enquiries that arrive after hours or on weekends?

**What happens next**
5. Walk me through the last enquiry you got. What did someone have to do with it?
6. Where do you record new jobs? A notebook, spreadsheet, job management software (e.g. ServiceM8, simPRO, Tradify, AroFlo, Fergus), or an email folder?
7. What details do you usually have to chase customers for before you can book or quote?
8. How do you reply? Phone call, email or text? Do you use any templates?

**Pain and priorities**
9. What goes wrong today? Missed enquiries, slow replies, double handling, urgent jobs buried in the inbox, or quotes going cold?
10. If enquiry handling worked perfectly, what would that change for you? (Let them answer in their own words. Don't suggest numbers.)
11. Have you tried any automation or AI tools before? What happened?

**Systems and decision**
12. Do you use Google Workspace (Gmail) or Microsoft 365 (Outlook)?
13. Who looks after your website, and could they add a form or a small piece of code?
14. Who else would be involved in deciding to go ahead?
15. Is there a time this needs to be working by, e.g. before the busy season?

## Trade-specific questions

### Electricians
- How do you currently spot **urgent or unsafe** jobs (no power, burning smell, sparking, tripping safety switches) among routine requests?
- What advice do office staff give callers about **electrical hazards** before a sparky arrives? Is there an approved script?
- What share of your enquiries are **quotes** (switchboard upgrades, EV chargers, solar and batteries, lighting) versus **faults** and **compliance** work (smoke alarms, test and tag, rental inspections)?
- Do you work with **real estate agents or property managers**? Who authorises the work and receives the invoice?
- Do you need customers to send **photos** (switchboard, install location) before quoting?
- Do you have an **after-hours emergency** service, and who's on call?

### Plumbers and gasfitters
- How are **burst pipes, flooding, blocked sewers and gas smells** handled when they come in by email or form rather than by phone?
- What do you tell customers who report a **gas smell**? (The Lead Assistant always flags this for an immediate call.)
- How much of your work is **hot water** (repairs versus replacements, heat pumps and rebates), **blocked drains**, **backflow testing** or **renovations**?
- Do you service **commercial** customers with scheduled compliance work, like backflow prevention testing?
- What information do you need before quoting a hot water replacement? (Household size, current system, location of the unit, photos.)

### Air-conditioning contractors
- How do you prioritise **"not cooling" calls in a heatwave**, especially for elderly or vulnerable occupants?
- What share of enquiries are **new installations** (split, ducted), **servicing and cleaning**, or **breakdowns**?
- What do you need for an install quote? (Room sizes, number of rooms, single or double storey, existing ducting, photos of the outdoor unit location.)
- Do you have **commercial service contracts** with scheduled maintenance?
- Do enquiries spike seasonally? What happens to response times in summer?

### Electronic security companies (alarms, CCTV, access control)
- How do you handle a **break-in or alarm failure** that comes in by email, versus your monitoring or emergency line?
- What share of enquiries are **new installations** (CCTV, alarms, access control, intercoms), **service or faults** (beeping panels, false alarms, offline cameras) or **monitoring**?
- What do you need before quoting? (Number of cameras or doors, site plan, remote viewing, existing equipment brand.)
- Are there **security-sensitive details** (alarm codes, site layouts) customers sometimes email? (Important for how we handle and store enquiries.)
- Do you need to verify who's asking before discussing an existing system?

## Qualification check (score while you talk)

| Factor | Strong fit | Weak fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enquiry volume | Steady written enquiries, e.g. 10+ per week across email and forms | Almost all by phone; very few written enquiries |
| Pain | Missed or slow enquiries, double handling, urgent jobs buried | "It's fine, just curious" |
| Systems | Google Workspace or Microsoft 365; a website with a form or a shared inbox | No business email, or only a personal mailbox they won't connect |
| Decision-maker | On the call, or easy to reach | Unclear who decides |
| Timing | Wants it working within 1–3 months | No timeframe |
| Expectations | Wants drafts for approval and safety flagging | Wants AI to reply automatically with no review |

- **Mostly strong:** suggest Basic (one source) or Standard (several sources, job management system, on-call alerts) and book scoping.
- **Mixed:** suggest a smaller first step, or an **optional** Business Improvement Assessment if they're unsure what to automate.
- **Mostly weak:** say honestly that it may not be worth it yet, and offer to stay in touch.

## Red flags and how to respond
- **Wants fully automatic replies with no human check:** explain that VERLONIQ designs for human approval, especially for safety-critical trades. If they insist, it isn't a fit for this service.
- **Wants guaranteed savings or ROI:** say you can't promise numbers without their data. Offer to measure before and after.
- **Asks for financial, legal or tax advice** (e.g. pricing their jobs, structuring their business): that's outside VERLONIQ's scope. Refer them to their accountant or adviser.
- **Asks VERLONIQ to own their accounts or pay their subscriptions:** explain the [cost structure](07-cost-structure.md). Customers own their accounts.
- **Talks about scraping lead sites or emailing people without consent:** decline politely.

## What not to say
- "Our AI handles X% of enquiries", "it saves N hours a week", or "it never makes mistakes".
- Anything implying the website demo is live AI. **It's a simulation.**
- Prices before scoping, beyond "we'll give you a fixed quote after scoping".
